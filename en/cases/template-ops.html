Trend- and Data-Driven Template Operations

Product
VerseIn
Role
Product Manager
Dates
2026.06 – 2026.11

Project

VerseIn is built around templates: a user uploads one photo and a template turns it into a video. The platform has nearly 600 templates, with new ones every week.

  • New template ideas came from gut feel.
  • Homepage order was decided by hand, yet the first row got about 13× the exposure of the rows behind it, so ranking more or less decided whether a template lived or died.

Process

Trends decide what to make

  • Claude Code generates scheduled trend reports for Reddit, Instagram and Xiaohongshu, 120+ so far; a weekly job scrapes competitors’ most-liked lists and compares rank changes.
  • Popular short videos are first split into frames with captions, then broken down by cover, hook, first 3 seconds, emotion and what the user has to upload, to judge whether they are worth turning into a template.

One analysis that survived: a “reuse score” for a public template library

The goal was to pick templates worth building into our own product from a public AI template site. It has 3,259 templates, and its built-in “hottest” order is views plus likes; views are about 60× likes, so it is really just sorting by exposure. I had AI write scripts to pull all the public data and re-score it on six dimensions:

The two orders barely overlap: of the site’s top 50 “hottest” and the top 50 by reuse score, only 1 is the same. The score produced 55 candidates (a cap per category, near-duplicate prompts collapsed to one); after manual review I picked 10 and rewrote each as a prompt with slots:

Data decides what to promote or pull

Three data sets side by side: backend config (model, price, input, tags), the event funnel (impression to share), and sampled frames (6 per preview). Comparing templates by mechanic, the key variable turned out to be nothing like the intuition:

Same model, same input, and A even costs more. Frame by frame, the only difference was that A has a two-beat cause and effect: you poke, the figure gets angry.

From gut call to formula

Bayesian smoothing guards against small samples, and a 15% freshness boost applies within each row. Every two weeks a script re-ranks and writes to production, then reads the result back to check it.

Results

  • New miniature templates must include an interactive reaction; the “change the material” direction was disproven by data and stopped.
  • Wrote down 4 rules: include an interactive reaction; set the target metric before choosing still image or video; if uploads are high but generations low, cut the price before pulling the template; cap holiday content at 3 to 4 templates.
  • Homepage ranking went from a gut call to a repeatable, checkable process every two weeks.
