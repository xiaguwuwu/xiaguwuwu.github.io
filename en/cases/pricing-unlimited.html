Pricing and Subscription Benefits

Product
VerseIn
Role
Product Manager
Dates
2026.06 – 2026.10

Project

  • VerseIn connected several video-model providers at once: some bill in credits, some by the second, some by the clip. Each model in the backend had a single “coin cost” field, so once resolution or duration changed, price and cost no longer matched.
  • The company wanted “unlimited generation” as a selling point for subscribers, but video generation is expensive.

Process

Pricing

  • Built a pricing calculator: it converts each provider’s pricing into one cost basis, computes break-even and recommended prices by model, resolution and duration, and shows the margin live as you change the price.
  • In the backend, “coin cost” became “base price”, converted by resolution and duration multipliers; unavailable spec combinations can be flagged.

Unlimited-generation benefit

Studied the terms and user feedback of 9 products. None is truly unlimited: they all control cost with concurrency limits, model allow-lists, spec caps, time windows and channel exclusions. Users accept slow; they don’t accept not knowing what they are waiting for. So the design is:

  • Each subscription tier names the models it can generate with for free.
  • Resolution and duration are capped; options above the cap are visible but locked.
  • Free jobs wait in a queue and can switch to credits at any time to run right away; a free request never silently spends credits.

Iterating on data

RoundProblem foundChange
Round 2Users without a subscription never saw the benefit: zero active exposureAdded a benefit banner to the top bar and made the channel switcher three-state
Round 3Users with the benefit defaulted to the credit channel, which hid the benefitDefault to the free channel when the user has the benefit; show the full pricing page when they don’t; track paywall views by trigger

Results

  • The pricing calculator became the company’s single source for plan pricing and margin estimates.
  • After the benefit launched, shipped three rounds of changes based on event data; the queue experience later got its own round, see Free-Queue Wait Estimates.
