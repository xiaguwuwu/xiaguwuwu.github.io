Free-Queue Wait Estimates

Product
VerseIn
Role
Product Manager
Dates
2026.09 – 2026.10

Project

Subscribers can generate videos for free but have to queue. Releases are throttled by a daily budget and light users go first, so the queue is not first come, first served.

VersionChangeResult
V1Showed “N people ahead of you”Later users legitimately jumped ahead, the number went up, and users said “this is a scam”
V2Removed the count; time left only goes down“Estimated 5 hours, and after 5 hours there’s still 1 hour left”

Process

  • Fill in the data: the old dashboard only counted jobs that were released, so people who gave up were not in the denominator. First added tracking: promised time, actual release time, and when users left the queue and where they went.
  • Find the cause: compared promise with reality across a week of 692 release records. The p90 actual wait was 3.1× the promise and 30% of jobs were more than an hour late; of users who switched to credits, 85% decided within 5 minutes of seeing the estimate.
  • Look around: studied 7 products. Those with minute-level waits show no time at all, those that show a queue position all ran into trouble, and if you do show a number, too long is safer than too short.
  • Design: show only a range, with the upper bound calibrated at p90; once shown it can only narrow, never grow; on overrun say so plainly and offer to keep waiting or switch to credits; show the range before submission.

Results

  • After launch the upper bound held 92% of the time on monthly plans and 100% on annual.
  • Better display only cuts complaints; shorter waits need a bigger budget or moving expensive models out of the free channel. Both were raised with the business side at the same time.
