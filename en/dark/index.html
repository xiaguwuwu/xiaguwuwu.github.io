      What does an AIGC product manager actually do? A VerseIn example

      At VerseIn, I checked Reddit, Instagram, Xiaohongshu and competitors’ trending lists every day to see which videos were taking off, worked out the mechanic behind them, wrote prompts, ran them on models such as Seedance, Kling and Veo, and turned the good results into templates. After launch I read the event data. Two similar miniature-figure templates had share rates of 51.6% and 19.5%, and a 6× gap in attributed revenue. Comparing frames showed the difference was whether the little figure reacted to the user, and later topics were screened on that point. Ranking for the nearly 600 templates on the homepage also moved to a formula over event data, re-run every two weeks.

      How do you use data to make product decisions?

      The free queue is one example. Users complained the wait was too long. I first added a queue dashboard and tracking, then pulled one week of 692 release records and compared the promised time with the actual wait: the p90 of actual waits was 3.1× the promise, so the first estimate was too optimistic. After switching to a conservative range calibrated on the 90th percentile, waits stayed within the upper bound 92% of the time on monthly plans and 100% on annual. Pricing is another: each upstream model had its own billing unit, so I converted them to one cost basis and worked out break-even and target-margin prices by model, resolution and duration. That calculator became the only data source the company uses to set plans and estimate margins.
